Keeping Your Glassboro Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Water heaters in and around Glassboro face a familiar set of stresses. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater in hard water needs annual descaling. And an older install getting a new heater often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early.
