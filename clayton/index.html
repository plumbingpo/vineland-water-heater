How Clayton Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
