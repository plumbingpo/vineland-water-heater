How Hammonton Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
